We call this intuition, and the word grants more authority than the phenomenon deserves. “Something feels wrong” may be the compressed residue of observations that never became explicit. It may also be anxiety, prejudice, wishful thinking, or a justification invented after the conclusion. Calling a judgment intuitive tells us about our inability to account for it and almost nothing about whether it is right. Still, a disagreement the system cannot explain may mark a limit of its representation before it marks a failure of the person’s reasoning.
